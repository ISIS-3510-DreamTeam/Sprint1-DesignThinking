Interview 1 — Situations (Music Discovery Problem)

| Situation | What? | How? | Why? | Who? |
|-----------|-------|------|------|------|
| 1 | The interviewee is at the gym, working out, and instead of listening to his usual music he starts switching between genres (electronic, rap, and others) while training. | He appears tired of his own musical routine, actively searching for new rhythms that give him back his drive; it's not a passive background activity but a deliberate exploration in the middle of physical effort. | Music likely serves an almost functional purpose for him (motivation, performance), not just accompaniment; when the music stops producing that effect, he feels the need to actively "refresh" it. This suggests his relationship with music is tied to mood and performance, not loyalty to a genre or artist. | A physically active person who trains regularly, a musical explorer by nature, linked to specific activities and moods. |
| 2 | The interviewee recounts that, upon seeing someone wearing headphones on the street, he wonders what they're listening to; he also spontaneously mentions viral videos and memes about this same phenomenon (asking strangers what they're listening to, or the contrast between someone's appearance and their music). | He speaks enthusiastically and recognizes the surprise as a pleasurable part of the experience ("oh, no, I never would have guessed"). It's not anxious curiosity, but playful. | The fact that he brings up social media content on the same topic, unprompted, suggests this curiosity isn't an isolated event but a broader cultural phenomenon he already identifies with and passively consumes. It's a need for connection or surprise stemming from the contrast between a person's image and their real tastes. | Someone who actively consumes entertainment content related to music on social media. |
| 3 | The interviewee shares songs only with his girlfriend and family. He avoids posting meaningful songs on social media and keeps the public playlists option disabled on Apple Music. | He describes himself as "jealous" and "selective" with the music that matters to him. There is a conscious care not to "burn out" certain songs by exposing them to just anyone or any context. | There is a tension between the desire to share (with his close circle) and the desire to protect (from strangers or casual contexts). He verbalizes it himself: he perceives music as something "intimate," and worries that others might have unfiltered access to everything he listens to, instead of only what he chooses to show. This reveals that, for him, a song's emotional value degrades when shared without control over context or audience. | A person with a well-defined intimate circle versus an undifferentiated external public. He understands music as a form of emotional intimacy, not just entertainment. |
| 4 | Upon hearing the app's proposal, the interviewee reacts with immediate interest, mentions concrete scenarios where he would use it (public transport, airports, trips), and then, when asked directly, expresses concerns about what information is shared, with whom, and whether nearby users could identify or contact him. | He moves from spontaneous enthusiasm to a more cautious, analytical stance when explicitly asked about the risks. He doesn't reject the idea, but conditions his interest on being able to control when it's activated and what it exposes. | The initial enthusiasm confirms that curiosity about other people's music (validated in Situation 2) is a real driver of interest in the app. But the later caution, combined with his behavior already observed in Situation 3 (selective privacy), suggests this isn't a new objection but an extension of an existing behavior pattern: he wants to discover and be discovered on his own terms, not automatically or passively. | A potential user genuinely interested in the product, but who will act as an active "gatekeeper" of his own information. Not a passive user who accepts default settings, but someone who will consciously evaluate privacy controls before adopting the app with confidence. |
